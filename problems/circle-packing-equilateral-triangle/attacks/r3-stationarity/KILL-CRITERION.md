## Gate 1 — theory gate

> *"If the FJ + core/rattler + wall-contact lemma cannot be written rigorously — in
> particular the treatment of **continua** of stationary points (rattlers, points sliding
> along a wall with one contact) — stop and write up where it broke."*

**Verdict: FIRED, in part — and the part that fired is the part that was flagged in
advance.**

* Parts (a), (b), (c) — existence, the Fritz John conditions written out concretely, and
  the structural prunes — **were** written rigorously; see README §§2–4, Lemmas 1–13. In
  this container the conditions are in fact stronger than Fritz John: the degenerate
  multiplier case is impossible (Lemma 4), so plain KKT holds at every maximiser with no
  constraint qualification. The exhaustiveness reduction approach **E** could not state is
  proved in README §5.
* Part (d) — rattlers and continua — **could not** be handled. README §6:
  * a point with no positive multiplier is entirely invisible to the first-order
    conditions, so each support carries a residual continuous completion problem in
    $2(n-m)$ free variables — the same covering/counting obligation recorded as *wall 2*
    on this board, now once per support;
  * the strata where the loaded system is under-determined, and on which interval
    Newton/Krawczyk therefore has nothing to contract, are **forced** for every sparse
    support ($|E| + W \le 2m$), and sparse supports are admissible and numerous. Excluding
    them needs either a second-order condition or a genericity assumption, and an
    exhaustiveness argument may not make a genericity assumption.

The two lemmas that would be needed, and that I could not prove, are stated explicitly as
(R) and (C) in README §6.3.

**Where it broke, in one sentence:** the exhaustiveness lemma is real and is delivered, but
it is not a reduction to a finite computation, because first-order conditions constrain
only the loaded points and say nothing about the rest.

## Gate 2 — enumeration gate

> *"Run the support enumerator at $n = 12$, just below $d(12) = 4+2\sqrt3$; if the
> admissible support count exceeds $\sim10^6$ or it does not terminate in an hour, record
> the count and stop."*

**Verdict: FIRED decisively, on both clauses.**

Counted exactly, up to graph isomorphism composed with the triangle's $S_3$ symmetry:

| loaded points $m$ | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| admissible support classes | 4 | 20 | 157 | 1 404 | 16 243 | 216 405 |

**Cumulative over $m \le 7$ alone: 234 233**, with successive growth ratios
$5.0, 7.9, 8.9, 11.6, 13.3$ — increasing. The loaded-graph layer at $m = 12$ (min degree 1,
max degree 6, $K_4$-free, $\le 24$ edges, all sound prunes) contains **1 241 462 173**
graphs, generated by `nauty-geng` in 515 s; each carries hundreds to thousands of wall
labellings, giving $\sim10^{11}$ support classes at $n=12$ on the measured wall factor.

The enumerator therefore also fails the termination clause: it cannot finish even the
$m=12$ graph layer within an hour, before any labelling or geometry.

Note (README §2, Lemma 2): the problem is scale-invariant, so "just below $d(12)$" and
"at $d(12)$" are the same enumeration; $d$ enters only through the side-capacity prune.

**Calibration performed before the count was reported** (a count from a wrong enumerator is
worse than no count): the supports of the known optima at $n=6,\,d=4$ and $n=5,\,d=4$ were
extracted from exact $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$ coordinates, their Fritz John multipliers
certified exactly, and both were confirmed **accepted** by the enumerator's prunes.

## Gate 3 — exclusion gate

> *"At $n = 12$ the known optimum's support must be found and verify feasible, while sampled
> others are excluded; if $>20\%$ come back undecided, record which strata and stop."*

**Not run — moot once gate 2 fired**, and the assignment says explicitly not to push on. The
calibration above is the part of gate 3 that could be discharged cheaply: the known optimum's
support *is* found by the enumerator at $n=5$ and $n=6$. README §6.2 predicts the rest of
gate 3's outcome without running it: the undecidable strata are the sparse supports with
$|E| + W \le 2m$, which are a large fraction of the enumeration, not a $20\%$ tail.

## Consequence for the board

Proposal V is `refuted` as a route to a lower bound at $n \ge 16$, and with it the
enumeration half of approach **E**. What survives is listed in README §8 — chiefly Lemma 4
(unconditional KKT in this container), Lemma 11 (extreme loaded points lie on the boundary),
and Lemmas 7/12/13 (boundary degree bounds), all of which are cheap filters usable by the
contact-graph and exact-algebraic work rather than by an exhaustion.
