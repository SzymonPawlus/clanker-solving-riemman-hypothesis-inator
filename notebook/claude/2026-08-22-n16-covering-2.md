# 2026-08-22 — n16 covering, round 2 (worker C1)

Task: beat the standing certified lower bound $a_{16}\ge 89267/20000 = 4.46335$ from
`attacks/n16-covering`, using 15 pieces of diameter strictly $<1$ covering $T_a$.

## What I found first, and it reframed the whole job

The predecessor's optimiser was a coordinate sweep on a fixed convex subdivision. I replaced it
with a **sequential-LP minimax solver** (`slp.py`) after noticing that, *for a fixed combinatorial
structure*, the problem is essentially a **convex program**:

- the constraint $Q(v_i-v_j)\le D^2$ (squared diameter of a face) is **convex** in the vertex
  coordinates and linear in $D^2$;
- the boundary conditions ($v=0$, $u=0$, $u+v=a$, corners pinned) are linear;
- the objective $\min D^2$ is linear;
- the **only** non-convex constraints are the orientation/convexity conditions
  $\mathrm{cross}(v_i,v_{i+1},v_{i+2})\ge0$.

So a trust-region SLP with an exact line search converges to the structure's true optimum, where a
coordinate sweep stalls on the non-smooth max.

**Result:** all five of the predecessor's independently-found subdivisions, *and* the plain
hexagonal $T_5$ cluster, converge to the identical value

$$a^\star=\frac{1}{D}=4.464101615137\ldots=\boxed{1+2\sqrt3}.$$

The predecessor's search was already at the structural optimum to 4 decimals; its plateau was not
optimiser weakness. At that optimum **all 15 faces have diameter exactly 1** (54 tight pairs
against 44 dofs — rigid), and the minimum convexity cross-product is $7.7\times10^{-3}$, i.e. the
non-convex constraints are **inactive**.

## The decisive negative experiment

Because the convexity constraints are inactive, I re-ran the same structure with them **deleted**
— a pure convex program, hence a global optimum, and an upper bound for *any* deformation of that
structure including ones with reflex vertices (i.e. overlapping convex hulls, the general
"cover by 15 convex sets" problem rather than "partition into 15 convex cells").

It returns **the same 4.4641016**, with **zero** reflex corners wanted.

So for this structure, allowing pieces to overlap buys exactly nothing, and the entire remaining
lever is the **combinatorial structure**. That killed a whole line of work cheaply.

## The coarse structure is forced

Let $4<a<5$ and let 15 sets of diameter $<1$ cover $T_a$.

- A set of diameter $<1$ meets a side of $T_a$ in a set of diameter $<1$, hence inside an interval
  of length $<1$. Each side has length $a>4$, so **each side meets $\ge5$ pieces**, and the total
  side-incidence count is $\ge15$.
- Let $n_1,n_2$ be the numbers of pieces meeting exactly one / exactly two sides ($\ge3$ is
  impossible at this inradius), and $n_{\rm int}=15-n_1-n_2$. Then $n_1+2n_2\ge15$ and
  $n_1+n_2=15-n_{\rm int}$, so $n_2\ge n_{\rm int}$.
- A piece meeting both $AB$ and $AC$ has every point within $2/\sqrt3=1.1547$ of $A$ (from
  $\alpha^2+\beta^2-\alpha\beta<1$), so the two-side pieces at a corner cover at most
  $[0,1.1547]$ of each side. With $n_2=3$ the middle $a-2$ of each side needs $\ge3$ further
  pieces, giving $n_1\ge9$ and hence $n_{\rm int}\le3$; with $n_2=6$ the same count forces
  $n_{\rm int}=0$ and the incentre (at distance $a/2\sqrt3>1$ from every side) is uncovered.

So the structure is **3 corner pieces + 9 edge pieces + 3 interior pieces**, exactly what the
certificate has. Moreover, with $B=15$ boundary edges, Euler plus the trivalence bound gives at
most $I=16$ interior vertices and $\sum_f \deg f = 75$ — and the existing structure already
attains both. Status: `sketch` (mine, unreviewed).

## What is left, and what I did about it

Only the fine adjacency. `comb.py` regenerates the geometry of *any* candidate structure from
scratch by a **Tutte barycentric embedding** (guaranteed convex faces, so a combinatorial move
never needs geometric repair — my earlier repair-LP failed on 40/40 flips, Tutte succeeds on
531/663), and `beam.py` beam-searches the flip neighbourhood, scoring each structure by its true
SLP optimum.
