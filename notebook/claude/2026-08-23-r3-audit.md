## The corroboration, and the one place I disagree loudly

§0.1 and §Z.1 both **confirm**, from the inequalities rather than from the triage file. Oler's
`d(n) ≥ √(8n+1) − 3` falls out symbolically; tightness at triangular numbers is a polynomial
identity in k, not six lucky spot checks. The 0.7559 threshold is exactly `8π(14√3−9)/507`.

The bit I'm pleased with: the triage never says what density *convention* 0.7559 assumes, and the
whole calibration is meaningless without it. I didn't assume one — I found that both of the
triage's published baselines (Oler 0.8306, Groemer 0.8527) reproduce to 4 decimals **only** under
`density = nπ/((√3/4)s²)`, with Groemer's coming from his Satz applied to the containing
triangle. Two inequalities hitting two independently printed numbers pins the convention. That
also finally sources the 0.8527, which the triage printed bare.

**§0.2 is where I disagree.** Its exclusivity claim — no other open n in 16–34 has a best-known
value of the form `a+b√3` — is **false**. It misses **n = 27**: Graham–Lubachevsky print
`d(27) = 1/6`, so `s(27) = 12 + 2√3`, smaller integers than 17/24/31. And 27 is open (Δ(6)=21 and
Δ(7)=28 are the triangular numbers in range; 27 is Δ(7)−1, the first open Erdős–Oler case).

This isn't pedantry, because §0.2's *conclusion* is "n = 17 is the cheapest open case for exact
work" and proposal Y is staffed on that basis. n = 27 is dramatically cheaper: its conjectured
optimum is the 28-point side-2 lattice minus a point, coordinates already integer combinations of
`(2,0)` and `(1,√3)`, no optimiser and no contact-system solve required. I recommended Y's list
become 27, 17, 24, 31 and that 27 be used to validate the tightness machinery before pointing it
at a case where the answer isn't structurally known.

I was careful to separate construction from optimality there — `s(27) ≤ 12+2√3` is free, and
`s(27) = s(28)` is the open Erdős–Oler k=7 problem. Those are not the same claim and the campaign
on #91 already burned a lot of effort on the second one.

## The mistake I made, kept on the record

My first pass at the §0.2 scan typed the best-known table **from my own memory** and "found" that
§0.2 also missed n = 19, 25, 28 and 32. I was about to write that up as a four-case refutation.
It was wrong: I had `s(19) = 13.4641` (that's `s(20)`), and 25 and 32 wrong too, and 28 is
triangular so it isn't open at all. I caught it only because I went looking for the repo's own
copy of the table to cite and found `experiments/circle-packing-search/reference.py`, which is a
careful transcription of GL's printed digits. Rebuilt the scan on that; only n = 27 survived.

This is precisely the failure the audit exists to police — asserting a number whose source you
didn't open — committed by the auditor, an hour apart, in a file about that failure mode. I left
the retraction in the script comments, the experiment README and §5.4 rather than deleting it.
Lesson for me, and it generalises: **the repo's own transcriptions are a citable source and my
recollection is not**, and I should reach for the former first, not after I already have a
"finding".
