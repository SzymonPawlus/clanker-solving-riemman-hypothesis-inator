# 2026-08-23 — r3-qsqrt3: exact tight $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$ certificates at $n = 17, 24, 31$

Worker `r3-qsqrt3`, branch `r3-qsqrt3`, round-3 approach **Y**. Everything below is `numerical`.

## Result

$s(17) \le 6+4\sqrt3$, $s(24) \le 8+4\sqrt3$, $s(31) \le 10+4\sqrt3$, exactly, with **tight**
certificates. All three reproduce the published Graham–Lubachevsky values; none beats anything.

Kill-criterion did not fire.

## How it went, in order

**Step zero paid for itself.** Re-deriving the closed forms against
`experiments/circle-packing-search/reference.py` at 40 dps turned up that $n = 24$'s published
$m = 0.174457630187010$ differs by 1 in its 15th significant figure from the value $8+4\sqrt3$
implies ($0.174457630187009439$). I had written a `> 14.5 digits` assertion and it fired. The right
test is "within one unit in the last *published* place", not a digit count, because the table's
precision is the binding constraint, not the closed form's. `circle-packing-ls/README.md` had
already recorded the same 1-ulp offset independently, which is reassuring. Two minutes of
irritation that would have been an hour of confusion later.

**The snap was easier than expected and the reason is interesting.** I anticipated needing to
solve the contact system. I did not: rescaling the LS float output by the conjectured
$d = a + 2\sqrt3$ and snapping coordinate-wise put 16/17, 24/24, 30/31 points onto
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$ with numerators $\le 10$ and residuals $\sim 10^{-15}$. These packings are
triangular-lattice blocks with a handful of defects, so the *coordinates* are in the field, not
just $s$. §0.2 of the round-3 triage claimed the field membership of $s$; the coordinate-level
membership is stronger and is what actually made this cheap.

I made the snap script prove its own unambiguity rather than assert it: for $(p+q\sqrt3)/2$ with
$|p|,|q| \le 80$, distinct values differ by at least $1/(80(1+\sqrt3)) = 4.6\times10^{-3}$, via
$|p+q\sqrt3| = |p^2-3q^2|/|p-q\sqrt3| \ge 1/(80(1+\sqrt3))$. Residuals of $10^{-15}$ against a
separation of $10^{-3}$ is a real argument; "it looked close" is not.

**The near-miss.** At $n = 17$ and $n = 31$, exactly one point per configuration refused to snap,
residual $\approx 3\times10^{-3}$ — above the proven separation. That is *precisely* what the
assigned kill-criterion looks like, and for a minute I thought it had fired. It had not: both are
rattlers (nearest-neighbour clearance $2.300$ and $2.134$; strict slack in all three walls). A
rattler has an open free region, so there is no exact position to snap *to* — the optimiser's
float value for it is where the billiard happened to stop. I replaced each with an independently
chosen exact point of its free region and let the same exact checker verify it.

The line I was careful about: this is only legitimate because the point is free. Had it been a
loaded contact, moving it would have been forcing the fit, and the criterion would have fired. I
wrote that distinction into `KILL-CRITERION.md` with the measurements, because "it's a rattler" is
the kind of claim that is easy to assert and easy to be wrong about, and a reviewer should be able
to re-measure rather than trust me.

**Tightness was the cheap part, which surprised me.** In the fixed placement the two
$d$-independent constraints drop out and $\sqrt3(d-x)-y \ge 0$ gives $d \ge x + y\sqrt3/3$, so
$d_{\min} = \max_i(x_i + y_i\sqrt3/3)$ exactly — a max over $n$ field elements, not an
optimisation. All three came back equal to the declared $d$. The existing
`circle-packing-ls/certificate.py` inflates $s$ by $\approx 10^{-11}$ because it snaps to
*rationals*, which cannot represent these coordinates; working in the right field removes the
inflation rather than shrinking it. That is the actual content of approach Y and it is a field
choice, not a numerical improvement.

## Discipline notes

- Validated before asserting (`RULES.md` §6): triangular lattice at $n = 3,6,10,15,21$ built from
  first principles, plus $n = 12$ (settled, $s = 4+4\sqrt3$) run through the *whole* pipeline as
  an end-to-end control. Then four negative controls, including one that must come back "feasible
  but not tight" — a checker that only ever says yes verifies nothing.
- `run_all.py` re-parses the emitted JSON *from disk* and re-checks it, so what is verified is the
  artifact and not an in-memory object. There is a guard that rejects decimal strings in exact
  fields (`RULES.md` §2 bans them, and the ban is easy to violate by accident via `str(float)`).
- Certificates stay in `experiments/`. They are `numerical`; `results/` takes only assumable
  claims, and this round is out of scope for it regardless.
- No float is consulted in any accept/reject decision. Floats appear in exactly two places:
  candidate pre-filtering for the rattler search (every candidate is then re-checked exactly), and
  the diagnostic prints.

## What I am least sure of

Not the arithmetic — it is short, exact, and has negative controls. The exposure is **convention**.
If I have read the triangle placement or the meaning of `side_length` differently from
`problems/circle-packing-equilateral-triangle/README.md`, everything above is internally
consistent and externally wrong, and no amount of exactness inside my own frame would show it.
That is the failure mode problem `RULES.md` §3's independent reimplementation exists for, and it
is why this is `numerical` and why I did not go near `results/`.

## For whoever picks this up

- The contact graphs at $n = 17, 24, 31$ are now **exactly** known (26, 45, 59 contacts at distance
  exactly 2). Approach V wants exact contact graphs; these are free input.
- The method transfers to any $n$ whose optimum lies in a small field. It does **not** transfer to
  $n = 16$, which is the one case in $16..34$ with no such closed form — a cheap negative signal
  that is consistent with $n=16$'s resistance without explaining it.
- If someone wants `verified:lean`: $n = 24$ is the best target. No rattler, so no free choice to
  justify, and $\binom{24}{2} = 276$ inequalities over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$ is a finite conjunction
  a Lean file can carry.
