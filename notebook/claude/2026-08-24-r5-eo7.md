# 2026-08-24 — r5-eo7: proposal AE, certified lattice-count at k = 7

## What I set out to do

Execute round-4 proposal AE (ranked first): turn "$\max_\Lambda|\Lambda\cap T(a)|\le 26$
for $a<6$" into a certified finite theorem, and name the one hypothesis that would take
it to EO(7).

## What happened, in order

1. **Read the campaign first.** `eo-counterexample-hunt` §6b had already scanned rotated
   lattices and §7 gives the counterexample window $a \in [5.9974, 6)$ (conditional on
   best-known records). That told me the interesting regime is $a \to 6^-$ and that the
   whole question is about the *strict* inequality.
2. **Pinned the quantifier.** The parameter count only comes out at 3 if you quantify the
   right things away. Choosing $v_1$ = shortest vector and Lagrange-reducing gives lines
   spaced $h \ge \sqrt3/2$ with points spaced $r > 1$ along them; then the bound depends
   only on (direction, spacing, offset). That also made the theorem *stronger* than AE
   asked for — it never uses "lattice", only "on equally spaced parallel lines".
3. **Measured before trusting.** Reproduced AE's 22 from scratch (max lattice count at
   $a = 6-10^{-6}$), then certified it in exact $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$ with a Pythagorean
   rotation $(493,276,565)$ at $a = 5999/1000$.
4. **Certified.** Interval B&B over $(\varphi,\kappa,\rho)$: $\le 25$ at $a=6$ in 175
   boxes, under a second. Validated at $k=4,5,6$; $k=3$ fails (zero slack there).
5. **Then killed my own Half 2.** The counting bound is discontinuous at $\delta = 0$:
   $\delta = 10^{-9}$ already takes it from 24 to 27. So the conditional theorem needs
   *exact* collinearity, which no packing has.

## Three things worth carrying

**The gain is entirely an equality-case artifact.** $\sum(\ell_j+1) = 28$ at the aligned
lattice — the true value at $a=6$. Everything from 28 down to 22 is the six chords being
*exactly* integers so that $c(\ell)=\lceil\ell\rceil$ rather than $\lfloor\ell\rfloor+1$.
That is why the bound is fragile and why the fragility cannot be tuned away. Any future
lower-bound tool in this repo that gets its margin from an equality case should be
checked for the same discontinuity *before* it is written up.

**Slack grows with $k$ here.** 0, 0, 1, 1, 2 for $k=3..7$. That is the opposite of every
other lane in this directory, where $k$ (or $n$) makes things worse. The reason is that
Lemma A gives $\Delta(k-1)+1$ against a target of $\Delta(k)-2$, and the difference is
$k-3$. It is the first thing I have seen here that gets *easier* as the case gets bigger,
and it is worth remembering when choosing targets.

**The bug that nearly became a finding.** My first run reported 17 000 stalled boxes and
"stalls at 27". I was one paragraph from writing that down as the measured wall. The real
cause: the $(\varphi,\kappa,\rho)$ box contains points with $\rho d_1 \ge h$, which are
not configurations at all. With the feasibility filter the same run closes in 129 boxes.
Exactly PROTOCOL-R5 §6 — the arithmetic was fine, the premise was not. I got lucky that
the stalled boxes clustered at one corner and were cheap to look at; if they had been
spread out I would probably have believed the wall.
