## What I actually learned, in order of how much it changed my mind

### 1. The brief's Voronoi plan is dead on arrival, and the numbers say why immediately

The instruction was: take the near-optimal 16-point configuration, coarsen its Voronoi diagram to
15 pieces. I computed the diagram (`enemy.py`) before writing any optimiser. At $a=4.6247636$:

- 13 of the 16 Voronoi cells already have diameter $>1$ — largest $1.4846$ ($P_{15}$);
- four cells have **area above $\pi/4 = 0.7854$**, so by the isodiametric inequality no single
  set of diameter $<1$ can cover any of them, whatever its shape.

So the merge step never gets to happen. The underlying reason is a clean bulk fact I had not
appreciated: a 1-separated point set has density $2/\sqrt3$ per unit area, while diameter-$<1$
hexagonal tiles have density $8/(3\sqrt3)$. **The covering wants $4/3$ as many cells as the
packing has points.** The two lattices differ by spacing $1$ vs $\sqrt3/2$; the packing's Voronoi
diagram is simply not the covering's dual. Every subsequent design decision followed from this.

### 2. The rattler

$P_4$ has exactly one contact ($P_4P_{11}$) and no wall contact; total degree 1 against 3–4 for
everything else. The other 15 points are isostatic (21 contacts + 10 wall contacts = 31, minus
$P_4$'s one = 30 constraints for 30 dof). So there is exactly one rattler and it carries the one
floppy mode. Reported, not touched (problem `RULES.md` §5).

### 3. The binding obstruction is the three corners, not a pair

`overlay.py` drops the enemy configuration rescaled into $T_{a^\star}$ (min distance $0.96526$)
onto my certified partition. Four pieces swallow $\ge2$ points, and three of them are the corner
pieces — each takes the corner point **and both of its edge neighbours**. Piece 4 (an edge
pentagon) takes $P_1,P_5$. So the "weak pair" the brief asked me to find is not a pair: it is the
corner triples $\{P_7,P_8,P_{13}\}$, $\{P_0,P_{10},P_{12}\}$ and $\{P_6,P_{14}\}$. That is the
same mechanism as $n=4$, where the method is exactly tight and each kite holds a corner plus the
centroid.

### 4. The combinatorial type of the optimum is forced, and I can prove it

Every seed family — random, $p$-centre, triangular lattice, enemy-derived — converges to
**3 corner quads + 9 edge pentagons + 3 interior hexagons**, $C_3$-symmetric, 31 vertices.
That is not luck. A piece meets a side in a set of diameter $<1$, so more than $a$ pieces meet each
side, i.e. $\ge5$ for $4<a<5$; hence $2B_2+B_1\ge15$ and therefore
$$I = 15-B_1-B_2 \le B_2 .$$
At most three interior pieces. `verify.py` confirms the certificate realises it: the boundary chain
is gapless with exactly 5 segments per side.

Area audit at $a^\star$ against the hexagonal optimum $3\sqrt3/8 = 0.649519$:
corners $0.49996$ ($95.5\%$ of their own $\pi/6$ ceiling), interior hexagons $0.642/0.648/0.654$
($99.0\%$), edge pentagons mean $0.5761$ ($88.7\%$). **The interior is already perfect; all the
slack is on the nine edge pieces.** If anyone continues this line — including the non-convex and
curved-piece workers — that is where to aim.

## Where I corrected the inherited framing

- The $3\sqrt3/8$ area figure is an *achieved* hexagonal density, not a proved cap. With only the
  rigorous inputs (corner piece $<\pi/6$; every piece $<\pi/4$) the accounting gives $a<5.038$ —
  **no proved wall below the ceiling**. The manager flagged this independently and is right.
- Even assuming the hexagonal bound, the accounting gives $a\le4.65194$, still above $4.6247636$.
  The honest statement is: reaching the ceiling needs all twelve non-corner pieces at $98.7\%$ of
  hexagonal efficiency, nine of them pinned to a straight side. Measured best: $88.6\%$.
- Certificates are reported as $a/\mathrm{diam}_{\max}$ throughout, so the dilation bound the
  manager pointed out is already taken.

## Mistakes I made

1. **Sign error in the power-diagram bisector.** I kept the wrong half-plane, so nearly every
   Voronoi cell came out empty and the $m=8$ control initially "converged" at $\delta=0.5$ —
   the four-piece answer. Caught only because $0.5$ was suspiciously round and 300/300 random site
   sets were being rejected. Lesson: instrument the rejection reasons, don't just count successes.
2. **`pkill -f "run2.py"` matched its own shell** and killed the command that was writing the
   patch, three times, silently losing edits. Use `pgrep` from a script file whose command line
   does not contain the pattern.
3. I let the $m=16$ run report $4.46388$ for a while before checking its face areas; one piece had
   area $0.324$ and the diameters were spread $0.9858..1.0000$. It was a stuck search, not a
   measurement. Reported as inconclusive rather than as a number.
