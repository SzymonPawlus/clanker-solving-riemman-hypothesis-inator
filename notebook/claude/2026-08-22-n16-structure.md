# 2026-08-22 — why the covering plateau sits at 1+2*sqrt3 (manager lane)

Subagent capacity was exhausted mid-round (session API limit), so I took the
question myself rather than idle: the campaign had recorded the plateau at
1+2*sqrt3 four separate times and never explained it.

The answer turned out to be one identity. Classify covering pieces by how many
sides of T_a they meet -- the classes are disjoint, so their lower bounds add.
Corners force 3, side middles force 9, and the deep triangle (points at
distance >= 1 from all three sides, side delta = a - 2*sqrt3) forces 3 as soon
as delta >= 1. And delta = 1 exactly when a = 1 + 2*sqrt3.

So the record is the least side length at which 15 pieces are *necessary*. The
searches are not stuck; they are sitting on the first point where what they
achieve equals what is forced.

Two things I nearly got wrong, both caught before anything was written down:

1. I reached for Borsuk's planar constant sqrt3/2 for "3 pieces cannot cover
   T_delta", which would have put the 16-piece threshold at 8*sqrt3/3 = 4.6188
   -- *below* the best-known packing at 4.6248, i.e. an apparent proof that the
   covering method can never settle n=16. It is wrong: sqrt3/2 is extremal for
   the disk and the Reuleaux triangle, and the equilateral triangle splits far
   better, into 3 parts of diameter delta/sqrt3 (join the centroid to the three
   side midpoints). The correct threshold is delta >= sqrt3, i.e. a >= 3*sqrt3
   = 5.196, and the striking result evaporates. This is the FINDINGS pattern
   exactly: a remembered constant that produced a dramatic conclusion, and the
   dramatic conclusion is what should have made me check it first.
2. Covering a segment of length L by intervals of length strictly < 1 needs
   floor(L)+1, not ceil(L) -- they differ precisely at integer L, which occurs
   in range at a = 3 + 4/sqrt3. Fixed and tested at L = 1 and L = 3 exactly.

The theorem also settles the V3/R1 disagreement without picking a side: R1 is
right that the n_1 >= 9 step fails (its witness is exact and I reproduced it),
V3 is right that the n_2 case split is not exhaustive, and these are different
steps. Theorem N repairs both by never splitting on n_2 and by using the right
corner constant 2/sqrt3 in place of 1 -- R1's witness sits at alpha = 1.05,
inside the 2/sqrt3 corner zone, so it no longer touches the middle.

What it does not do: break the plateau. Counting cannot force a 16th piece
until a = 3*sqrt3, far above the target. The lane's honest next step is to feed
the now-forced class counts into the area budget of n16-covering-limit's Lemma
S, which computed its ceiling without knowing them.
