# 2026-08-26 — r5-bnb24: Markot-class active-region branch and bound (proposal AI)

Worker `r5-bnb24`, branch `r5-bnb24`. Optimality side only; no packing produced.

## What I was asked

Port the Markot–Csendes-class interval branch and bound, targeting `n = 24` rather than
`n = 16`, with **per-point active-region / area-reduction propagation** as the new
ingredient — the thing the repo's own dyadic B&B (issue #28 / PR #56) lacked and the
reason it drowned at `n = 16, d = 8` once one-point-per-cell forcing evaporated. Hard
calibration gate at `n = 12`, where `d(12) = 4 + 2√3` is `cited`: beat PR #56's
`d(12) > 6.95` on both sides or stop.

## Design decisions, and why

1. **Cells, not lattice points.** `attacks/r3-gridmis/` snaps a hypothetical packing to a
   triangular lattice and refutes by `alpha(G) < n`. I use the dyadic *partition* of
   `T_d` instead: a packing point lies in some closed cell, cells of side `< 2` hold at
   most one point, and two cells holding two points have maximum separation `>= 2`. Same
   shape of lemma, but the cells carry a **hierarchy**, which is what the propagation and
   the capacity bound both need. `r3-gridmis`'s vertex set has no such structure.

2. **The wall is combinatorial, so attack the branching, not the arithmetic.** PR #56
   branches a cell's points over its four children *independently per cell*, so it
   enumerates the product over cells of sub-positions. At `n = 12, d = 7` that product is
   `4^12` per level of refinement. The fix is to (a) never resolve one cell fully before
   the others, and (b) propagate every partial decision globally.

3. **Three propagators.** Tile forcing (`#live tiles == #points left` ⟹ all occupied),
   active-region arc consistency (delete every cell that conflicts with *every* cell of
   an occupied tile's active region — precomputed AND-of-neighbourhoods per dyadic
   subtree makes this one bitset AND), and a hierarchical Oler capacity bound. All three
   run to fixpoint at every node.

4. **No symmetry reduction was used at all.** I could have quotiented the root split by
   the `D3` action on the three level-1 corner cells (an exhaustive relabelling argument,
   worth a factor `<= 6`), and PR #56 does exactly that. I did not, because it buys a
   constant and problem `RULES.md` §5 makes symmetry claims the easiest thing in this
   directory to get subtly wrong. Every number reported here is from an unrestricted
   search over the full configuration space.

## What happened

The calibration gate cleared, from below and from above, at a *coarser* level than the
incumbent needed: `d(12) > 7.1` at level 5 (1024 cells) in 4.4e6 nodes, against PR #56's
`d(12) > 6.95` at level 6 with 7.7e6 nodes and a timeout at 2.4e8 nodes for `d = 7.0`.
`r3-gridmis` reached `7.0` with glucose4. So the propagation is worth roughly a level of
resolution plus a factor 50 in nodes at the incumbent's own hard point.

At `n = 24` the story is the honest one: Oler already gives `d(24) >= 10.8924`, which is
95.0 % of the anchor `8 + 2√3 = 11.4641`, and my search does not reach it. Details and
the exact frontier are in `attacks/r5-bnb24/README.md`.

- That the solver never returns UNSAT at any `d >= d(n)`.
