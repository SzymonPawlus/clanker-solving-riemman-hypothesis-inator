## What I was asked

Two things, in order.

1. Re-derive, not accept, the ideation lens's claim that after $S_n$ reduction the level-2 moment
   matrix at $n=16$ (dense $561\times561$) splits into blocks of size $9,9,3,1$; level 3
   (dense $6545$) into blocks $\le 31$; invariant scalar moments $56$ at degree $\le4$ and $275$

2. Measure the *slack* of the dense relaxation against known $d(n)$.

## 1. The size claim — confirmed, in full

I built `experiments/packing-r3-sdpgate/symmetry_sizes.py` from scratch rather than reading
anyone's arithmetic. Route: the monomials of degree $\le L$ in $x_1..x_n,y_1..y_n$ form a
permutation module for $S_n$ (index permuted simultaneously in $x$ and $y$); the isotypic block
sizes of the symmetry-adapted SDP are the multiplicities $m_\lambda$; so compute the permutation
character and pair it with the irreducible characters.

The permutation character has a pleasant closed form I derived independently: a monomial with
exponent pairs $(a_i,b_i)$ is fixed by $\sigma$ iff $(a_i,b_i)$ is constant on each cycle, so the
number of fixed monomials of degree $\le r$ is $\sum_{k\le r}[q^k]\prod_{c}(1-q^{\ell_c})^{-2}$
over the cycles $c$ of $\sigma$. Irreducible characters by Murnaghan–Nakayama on beta-sets;
multiplicities by exact `Fraction` arithmetic, asserted integral.

Everything matched, including the lens's own consistency check
$\sum_\lambda m_\lambda \dim\lambda = \binom{2n+L}{L}$:

- level 2, $n=16$: $561 = 9\cdot1 + 9\cdot15 + 3\cdot104 + 1\cdot105$, i.e. blocks $9,9,3,1$ on
  $\lambda = (16),(15,1),(14,2),(14,1,1)$.
- level 3, $n=16$: $6545$, blocks $31,23,15,9,4,2$ — max $31$, exactly as claimed.
- invariant scalar moments: $56$ at degree $\le4$, $275$ at degree $\le6$ (dense $58{,}905$ and
  $2{,}760{,}681$).

I also verified by hand, independently of the script, before running it: the trivial multiplicity
at level 2 is just the number of $S_n$-orbits of degree-$\le2$ monomials, which are
$1;\,x_i;\,y_i;\,x_i^2;\,y_i^2;\,x_iy_i;\,x_ix_j;\,y_iy_j;\,x_iy_j\,(i\ne j)$ — nine of them; and
the invariant-moment generating function $\prod_{k\ge1}(1-q^k)^{-(k+1)}$ gives cumulative
$1,3,9,23,56,126,275$, so $56$ and $275$. Two routes, one answer.

**One correction, small.** "Constraints collapsing to two orbits" needs $D_3$ as well as $S_n$.
Under $S_n$ alone the $3n$ containment constraints are **three** orbits (one per triangle edge),
so it is $1+3 = 4$; the $D_3$ that rotates the edges merges them into $1+1 = 2$. Nothing rides
on it — the proposal does say $S_n \times D_3$ elsewhere — but it should be stated precisely.

**Bonus observation nobody asked for.** The block sizes and the invariant-moment counts are
*independent of $n$* for $n \ge 2L$: $9,9,3,1$ at level 2 and $31,23,15,9,4,2$ at level 3, for
$n = 8, 12, 16$ alike. That is the strongest form of the lens's point — the reduced SDP does not
grow with $n$ at all. It also makes the negative result below sharper, not weaker.

## 2. The strength gate — the relaxation is worthless

`moment_gate.py` builds the dense level-$L$ moment relaxation of approach C's program verbatim.
I validated the machinery *before* the main runs (repo `RULES.md` §6): the textbook
$\max x$ s.t. $1-x^2\ge0$ at levels 1 and 2, $\max x_1x_2$ on the simplex (exact $1/4$), and —
the real test — the packing program itself at $n=2$ and $n=3$, where $f = 1$ exactly, run with a
*loosened* cap $t \le 4$ so that the cap could not supply the answer. Both returned $1.000000$.
So the code is right and the relaxation is exact at $n = 2,3$.

Then it falls off a cliff. Level-2 relative slack in $d$ is 38–71 % across $n = 4\ldots12$.
The gate said "a few percent"; approach C said 1 %.

The diagnosis is the good part. The level-2 values are, to solver precision, exactly

$$f_2(n) = \frac{2n}{3(n-1)},$$

which is *precisely* the elementary bound "min $\le$ mean": $\sum_{i<j}\lVert p_i-p_j\rVert^2$ is
convex in each point, hence maximised at the vertices, and with $a,b,c$ points at the three
vertices of the unit triangle it equals $ab+bc+ca \le n^2/3$. Divide by $\binom n2$. So the whole
level-2 hierarchy — 20,000 moment variables, a $351\times351$ PSD block — reproduces a
two-line variance argument and nothing else. Its $d$-bound *increases to $\sqrt6 = 2.449$ and
stops*, while $d(n)$ grows like $\sqrt{8n}$. At $n = 16$ it would give $2.37$ against Oler's
$8.358$ and a best-known $9.25$.

Level 3 buys a little and is nowhere near enough (see the attack write-up for the numbers).

## What I actually believe, and what I do not

- The size re-derivation is exact integer arithmetic and self-checking; I am confident in it.
  Status `numerical` all the same — it is a computation.
- The SDP numbers are floats from SCS/CLARABEL, several returning `optimal_inaccurate`. They are
  **hypotheses about bounds**, not bounds, and I have asserted no bound on $d(n)$ anywhere. For a
  *negative* gate this is fine and expected: I am arguing the relaxation is too weak, and solver
  error of $10^{-7}$ does not rescue a 40 % gap.
- The thing I am least sure of: whether $f_2(n) = 2n/(3(n-1))$ *exactly* or merely to seven
  digits at the $n$ I ran. I have not proved the identity; I matched it numerically at
  $n = 4,5,6,7,8,10,12$ and give the elementary argument for why that value is an upper bound.
  It does not change the verdict either way.
