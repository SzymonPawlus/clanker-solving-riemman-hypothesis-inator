# 2026-08-23 — r3-gridmis: grid-rounding to a finite independent-set refutation (approach W)

Worker `r3-gridmis`, branch `r3-gridmis`. Round-3 proposal **W** from
`problems/circle-packing-equilateral-triangle/attacks/r3-approaches/README.md` §2.

**Outcome: the kill-criterion fired.** The method is sound, calibrates cleanly at $n=12$, and
produces externally checked refutations — but at $n = 16$ it tops out well below Oler's
$\sqrt{129}-3 = 8.3578$. Write-up: `attacks/r3-gridmis/`, code: `experiments/packing-r3-gridmis/`.

## What I did, in order

1. **Derived the perturbation lemma before writing any solver code**, as instructed. The two
   soundness traps named in the assignment are both real and both have a specific fix:
   - *Boundary.* Snapping can push a point out of $T_d$. Fixed by taking the vertex set inside the
     $r$-*relaxed* triangle. The proof step is one line of Cauchy–Schwarz, and it only works
     because the three half-planes are written with **unit** normals — with un-normalised normals
     the relaxation constant is wrong by the normal's length and the vertex set comes out too
     small, which is the unsound direction. That is the trap inside the trap.
   - *Collapse.* Two packing points could in principle snap to one vertex. They cannot, and the
     reason is the same inequality that gives the separation: $|v_i - v_j| \ge 2 - 2r = \rho > 0$.
     So "trap (a)" is not a case to handle, it is a case the hypothesis $r < 1$ excludes — which
     is why $r<1$ is a hypothesis of the lemma rather than a convenience.
   - Also noticed a free tightening: lattice distances are quantised
     ($|v-v'|^2 = \tfrac{g^2}{4}(\Delta a^2 + 3\Delta j^2)$), so the effective separation
     $\rho_{\rm eff}$ is the next realisable lattice distance $\ge \rho$, not $\rho$ itself. At
     $g = 1/4$ that is $\sqrt3 = 1.7321$ against $\rho = 1.7113$.

2. **Made every soundness-critical comparison exact.** Indexing lattice points as $(a,j)$ with
   $x = ga/2$, $y = gj\sqrt3/2$ puts $x$ in $\mathbb Q$ and $y$ in $\sqrt3\mathbb Q$, so squared
   distances are rational and all three containment tests reduce to rational comparisons after a
   sign-checked squaring. `covering_radius_bound` returns a *rational* $r \ge g/\sqrt3$ by an
   integer-`isqrt` ceiling and asserts $3r^2 \ge g^2$ before returning. Rounding $r$ **up** is the
   safe direction; I checked that explicitly rather than assuming it.

3. **Tested the lemma, not just the code.** `test_lemma.py` snaps 1 199 randomly generated
   feasible point sets and checks all three conclusions (in $V$, distinct, no edge). Zero
   failures. This is the test that would catch a wrong relaxation constant, and I wrote it before
   trusting any refutation.

4. **Two-sided calibration at $n=12$.** 25 instances at $d \in \{7.465, 7.5, 7.6, 8, 9\}$ and
   $g$ down to $1/12$ (up to 5 995 vertices): **all 25 returned a size-12 independent set**, each
   re-verified exactly. Nothing above $d(12)$ was ever refuted. Below: refuted $d(12) > 7.0$
   ($93.8\%$ of the true $4+2\sqrt3$).

5. **Proof artifacts.** glucose4 via `pysat` with `with_proof=True`, checked by `drat-trim` built
   from Marijn Heule's source. Two gotchas worth recording: `cadical153`'s proof through pysat did
   not validate ("conflict claimed, but not detected") while glucose4/glucose3/lingeling did; and
   pysat omits the terminating empty clause, so the `.drat` file needs a final `0` line appended
   or drat-trim reports "no conflict". Both are now handled in `satproof.py`.

6. **Pushed at $n=16$** until the budget ran out.

## The thing I got wrong first, and the correction

My first ceiling estimate said the method could refute at most
$d < \tfrac{\rho_{\rm eff}}{2} d(n) - 2\sqrt3 r$ — the value you get if you forget that
independent sets are confined to the lattice. It is **false**, and the data refuted it within
minutes: at $n=12$, $g=1/6$ the method refuted $d = 6.8$, whose scaled side $7.78$ is *above*
$d(12) = 7.46$. The lattice restriction is a genuine extra gain (worth about 4% at $n=12$, about
2% at $n=16$), because a continuous $\rho$-separated $n$-set in the relaxed triangle need not have
a lattice representative. Good reminder that the "obvious" scaling argument had a direction error
in it, and that the cheap empirical check was worth more than the derivation.

## Why it dies (the honest version)

Closing a gap of $\epsilon = d(n) - d$ needs $g = \Theta(\epsilon)$, hence $\Theta(\epsilon^{-2})$
vertices and $\Theta(\epsilon^{-4})$ edges — that is the quadratic-cost/linear-gain trade the
assignment asked me to quantify. But the size is the smaller half of the problem. The real
obstruction is that as $g \to 0$ the lattice gain vanishes, so the discrete instance *converges to
the original continuous problem* while blowing up. Refuting $d$ near $d(16)$ means asking a
general-purpose CDCL solver, which knows no geometry, to settle an instance arbitrarily close to
the open question. Measured: $n=16$, $g=1/5$, $d=8.0$ (861 vertices) took 75 s; the extrapolated
requirement to clear the Oler floor is $g \approx 1/7$, roughly 1 800 vertices, and the observed
growth in solve time between neighbouring $d$ is a factor of several per $0.2$ in $d$.

## Cross-checks that mattered

- The exhaustive branch-and-bound (`mis.py`) and glucose4 agreed on **every** instance where both
  finished — two independent search procedures, one written here and one not.
- glucose4 was decisively stronger on the hard end: $n=12$, $g=1/8$, $d=7.0$ was UNKNOWN to my
  B&B after $10^6$ nodes and UNSAT to glucose4 in 115 s. I would have under-reported the method's
  reach by a full grid level if I had trusted my own solver alone.

## What I am least sure of

Lemma 1 is prose I wrote and nobody has checked. The step I would attack first is (ii), the
boundary step: it is correct as written *given* unit normals, and I verified the normalisation by
hand and the resulting predicate against an independently written float predicate — but it is
exactly the kind of step where a factor of 2 or a $\sqrt3$ hides. Second would be the claim that
the max–min-vertex-distance over an equilateral cell is the circumradius; true, and standard for
acute triangles, but stated without proof.

## Not done / left for whoever picks this up

- No symmetry breaking. The configuration has the triangle's $S_3 \times \mathbb Z_2$ symmetry
  when $d/g \in \mathbb Z$; worth maybe a factor of 6 and I did not spend the budget on it.
- No geometric pruning inside the SAT search (the encoding is deliberately trivial so the encoder
  is auditable — but that is also why it is weak).
- No LRAT/`cake_lpr` upgrade; drat-trim is itself unverified software, which is a fair objection
  to the "externally checked" framing.
