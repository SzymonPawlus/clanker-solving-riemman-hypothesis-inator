## What I expected to do, and what I actually did

The assignment set this up as a rounding exercise: pull the float dual out of HiGHS, round to
rationals, verify in `Fraction`, repair if it is infeasible by a hair, and record the obstruction
if the denominators blow up. I never got there, because looking at the constraint geometry first
made the float solver unnecessary.

The step that changed the shape of the task: rewrite the LP in the coordinates
`g = (√3/4)c_A`, `h = 3c_L`. Oler's member is `(g, h) = (1/2, 3/2)`, and for a configuration on
which Oler is **tight** the constraint becomes

```
s(K)·(g − 1/2) + (h − 3/2) ≥ 0,     s(K) = 4√3·A_K/M_K.
```

Every tight configuration's boundary line passes through the *same point* — Oler's. So the binding
constraints form a pencil through Oler, the feasible set is a cone with apex there, and the
objective `a²g + ah = a·(ag + h)` is a positive multiple of `ag + h`, which is a convex combination
of two constraints as soon as two available slopes bracket `a`. The dual is then written down, not
solved for:

```
λ = (s₂ − a)/(s₂ − s₁),   y₁ = 3aλ/M₁,   y₂ = 3a(1 − λ)/M₂.
```

Both dual constraints come out **tight** and the objective is exactly `(a² + 3a)/2 = n − 1`. The
side-`m` triangular lattice has slope exactly `m`, so consecutive lattices bracket every `a ≥ 1`.

I got this wrong once first: I computed the dual weights in the `(g, h)` coordinates and forgot the
`1/M` factor that converts back to the original constraint scaling. The check caught it
immediately — the dual objective came out `6a − 10 ≈ 15.07 > 15`, violating weak duality, which is
impossible for a feasible dual. That is exactly the sanity check I would not have had if I had
verified in floats with a tolerance: `15.07` vs `15` is far outside any tolerance, but a subtler
slip would not have been. Worth remembering that weak duality is itself a free consistency test on
the certificate, independent of whether the arithmetic is exact.

## What is established

- **Exact dual certificate: verified**, `n = 16, 17, 18`, twice each (tightest bracket, and a
  lattice-only bracket that lives inside the sibling's own library from refinement size 5). Checked
  in `Q(√(8n+1))` with `Fraction` coefficients. Both dual constraints hold with equality; the dual
  objective is exactly `n − 1`. **No rounding and no repair were needed.**
- **Kill-criterion did not fire.** Denominator sizes, the thing it asks about: two digits.
  `y₁ = −21/2 + √129`, `y₂ = 81/10 − (7/10)√129` at `n = 16`.
- **The collapse proposition closes.** (D) caps each free score variable pointwise by the *linear*
  member's value; (V) is a `+1`-coefficient sum, hence monotone; so raising every variable to its
  cap preserves (V) and keeps (D). The objective sees only `(c_A, c_L)`. Hence the free-score LP and
  the reduced LP have the same optimum. Evaluating (V) at the cap and using Euler gives literally
  the reduced constraint.
- **Reach.** The three dual relations were verified symbolically in `(a, r₁, M₁, r₂, M₂)`, so the
  certificate is an identity, not a fit. The collapse therefore holds at every `n`.
- **Bridge.** The sibling's LP is fed outward-rounded `A_up, M_up`, so my exact dual is not
  automatically theirs. Verified `A_up ≤ (1+ε)A`, `M_up ≤ (1+ε)M` with `ε = 10⁻³⁰`, so `y/(1+ε)` is
  dual-feasible for their relaxed LP; re-ran at an explicit *rational* side length so the whole
  bridge check is `Fraction`-only. Their LP's threshold in `d` exceeds Oler by at most
  `2·10⁻²⁹ + 10⁻⁴⁰`.

## The finding I did not expect

The proposition predicts the sibling's *whole* published table, including the two rows that sit
**above** Oler. Model: let `s_max` be the largest slope among the library's Oler-tight members. If
`a_Oler(n) ≤ s_max` the bracket exists and the LP returns Oler; if not, the optimum runs to
`c_A = 0` and `d = 4(n−1)/(3 + s_max)`.

- library size 3 → `s_max = 3` → `d(16,17,18) = 10, 32/3, 34/3` — the sibling reports
  `10.000000, 10.666667, 11.333333`.
- library size 4 → `s_max = 9/2` (from the 3×3 rhombus) → `n = 16, 17` bracketed, Oler;
  `n = 18` not, `d = 136/15 = 9.0666…` — the sibling reports `9.066667`.

All six match. I also re-ran their `reduced_lp` at sizes 3, 4, 6 myself as a float sanity check and
got exactly those numbers. So the "under-constrained library" rows are not noise; they are the
model's other branch, and the reason size 4 fails at `n = 18` is precisely that it lacks the side-5
lattice.

## The real reason it collapses

Sharper than "the nonlinearity gets suppressed", and this is the part I would want a reviewer to
attack hardest. (D) and (V) are used *only* through their consequence `c_A A_K + c_L M_K ≥ n_K − 1`.
Even the strictly larger family that replaces pointwise (D) by its summed form gives the same
consequence, because `Σ_f area(f) = A_K` exactly. So what pins the family is not the score at all —
it is that the conclusion is **affine in (area, perimeter) of the convex hull**. Lattices and rhombi
make every such bound tight simultaneously at Oler's coefficients and bracket every `a ≥ 1`.
Improving on Oler needs a conclusion of a different shape.

## What I am least sure of

proof that their *family* is what their README says it is. If their (D) were meant to be something
weaker than pointwise domination, my §2 proposition would be proving something about a smaller

Second: I did not check that the coded `±1e6` box bounds on the score variables are inactive at the
