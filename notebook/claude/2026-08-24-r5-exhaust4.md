## The finding

**Theorem.** For every level $L\ge2$, the node consisting of the nine cells containing the
$\Delta(4)$ lattice minus one point is refuted by none of the pair rule, the capacity rule, or the
Oler-hull rule. Proof in three lines each: multiplicities are 1 (cells are smaller than $1/3$);
$3\nmid 2^L$ so a lattice point is never a cell vertex, and a strictly convex function attains its
max over a triangle only at vertices, so `maxsep` strictly exceeds $1/3$; and the corner-deleted
hull is an **exact equality case of Oler** (area $2\sqrt3$, perimeter 8, bound exactly 9) while the
cell hull is strictly larger. Verified in exact arithmetic for $L\le12$; the Oler bound converges
to $9.0002$ at $L=16$ and never reaches 9.

**Proposition.** The dyadic pigeonhole closes EO($k$) iff $(k-1)^2 < (k^2+k-2)/2$, i.e. iff
$k\le3$. AF's pre-declared ceiling was $k=6$. It is $k=3$.

Together with `eo-exhaustion` §1.1 (which I re-derived and agree with) both branches are shut:
fixed rational sides terminate but by monotonicity can never reach the conjecture; the uniform
version would reach it but provably never terminates.

## What surprised me

* **Strictness only bites inside a piece.** Between two distinct cells the max separation always
  strictly exceeds the true distance, so the boundary case is destroyed. Inside a cell the diameter
  is attained, so $h\le t$ kills multiplicity 2. That single asymmetry is why $k=3$ closes and
  $k=4$ does not, and it is the cleanest statement of the "+1" I have seen in this repo.
* **The exhaustion localises beautifully and still fails.** The supportable fraction of $T(1)$
  falls 0.750 → 0.387 → 0.080 → 0.025 over levels 3–6, and at level 5 every supportable cell is
  within $0.11$ of a lattice point. AF's "finite occupancy profiles" really do appear. The
  neighbourhood just never becomes empty.
* **A $10^{-7}$ rounding bug nearly hid all of this.** My rational upper bound for $\sqrt q$
  over-estimated perfect squares by $10^{-7}$, which is exactly enough to stop the Oler-hull rule
  firing at the root for $n=10$ — the configurations that decide these cases sit on the rule's
  equality. Caught by the $\Delta(4)$ validation row, which is the entire argument for running a
  validation ladder before the target.
* **No triangle has capacity exactly 2**, because $a(2)=a(3)$. So any partition-and-capacity
  closure must use non-triangular pieces. I could not find that recorded anywhere in the repo and
  it widens the sibling covering lane's question in a way that might matter.
