## What I set out to do

Problem `RULES.md` §6 target 3: exact certificates for Graham–Lubachevsky's `22 <= n <= 34`.
The assigned idea was to use an interval Krawczyk operator to *prove a solution of the contact
system exists* in a rational box rather than solving for it in closed form, since the
`Q(sqrt 3)` route of `r3-qsqrt3` only worked because 17/24/31 happen to have closed forms.

## The decision that made the whole thing easy

Substituting `y = sqrt(3) u` shears the triangle so that **every constraint becomes rational**:

```
u >= 0        x - u >= 0        d - x - u >= 0
(dx)^2 + 3 (du)^2 >= 4          d_min = max_i (x_i + u_i)
```

No `sqrt(3)` enters the arithmetic at any point; it only reappears when a certificate is
written back in `(x, y)`, where `y = u*sqrt(3)` is exact in `Q(sqrt 3)`. I had been about to
carry `sqrt(3)` as an interval constant through the whole Krawczyk computation, which would
have worked but would have made every endpoint fatter and every bug harder to see. Worth
remembering for any future exact work in this container.

## The thing I nearly got wrong, twice

**First**: I printed `'%.30f' % float(Fraction)` for `n = 12` and read off
`7.464101615137754386...` against the exact `4 + 2*sqrt(3) = 7.464101615137754587...`. That
looked like a 2e-19 improvement on a *proven* value. It was a float-formatting artifact — the
`float()` cast throws away everything past 17 digits and `%.30f` prints the double's exact
binary value, which is not the rational's decimal expansion. Ten minutes of "have I broken a
proven case" for a printing bug. Rule for next time: never render a `Fraction` through
`float()`; go through `mpmath` at declared precision.

**Second, and the real one**: the assignment warned that "an enclosure of a stationary point is
not by itself a packing certificate". That is exactly right, and the reason is measurable:

> At **all 20** configurations I ran, the tight constraint set is **over-determined**,
> `K > rank`, with deficiency 1 to 24.

So the square subsystem must drop `K - rank` tight equations, each of which is an *active*
inequality at the solution. Over any box containing the solution its interval evaluation
straddles zero, so it can never be verified. The box can therefore never certify feasibility on
its own here. I checked the other two ingredients do hold — the non-tight constraints are
strictly satisfied across the whole box at all 20 `n` — which makes the failure specific rather
than vague.

The bound therefore comes from an explicit configuration: snap to denominator 1e40, restore
wall incidences exactly, then repair separation with a **homothety about `A = (0,0)`** by a
rational `lambda` with `lambda^2 >= 4/min_sep^2`. A homothety about `A` maps `T_d` to
`T_{lambda d}`, so containment survives for free and every separation is multiplied by
`lambda >= 1`. That single trick removes the "inflate `s` until feasible" search loop in
`circle-packing-ls/certificate.py` entirely, and it is unconditional: whatever the snapping
did, the scaling fixes it. Measured repair: `lambda - 1 ~ 1e-41`, exactly 1 at the lattice
cases.

## Result

Krawczyk contracted at all 20 `n` (7 calibration + 13 open), enclosure widths ~1e-49. All 20
got exact, exactly-verified, **tight** certificates — the first tight ones in this repo outside
the three `Q(sqrt 3)` cases, and 29 orders of magnitude closer than `certificate.py`'s ~1e-11
inflation. Of the 15 `n` with a published Graham–Lubachevsky entry, 13 reproduce it, 2 (`n = 33, 34`)
are above it because the best float candidate available is a worse local optimum, and **none is
below** — so `RULES.md` §4 escalation never triggered. The other 5 are calibration cases
checked against exact closed forms.

## Two process notes

- **Error bars matter.** GL print `d(n)` to 15 s.f., so the implied `D = 2/d(n)` is an interval
  of width ~4e-14. At `n = 25` my certified value sits 8e-15 *below* the midpoint, and at `n = 29` 3.4e-14
  below it. Compared
  against a single rounded float that reads as a record; compared against the band it is a
  match, which is what it is. I built the band comparison before looking at the numbers, which
  is the only reason I did not spend an hour on it.
- **The independently-written checker earned its keep.** `packing-r3-recheck/recheck.py`, by a
  different worker straight from the problem statement, accepts all 20 certificates and agrees
  they are tight. Same model family, so no promotion — but it is the control that would have
  caught a convention error, and convention is what I flagged as my own weakest point.

## Least sure of

The tight-structure identification (a float tolerance of 1e-7, with a retry sweep). A wrong
structure sends Newton somewhere else. The exact final check would reject an infeasible result,
and `lambda - 1 ~ 1e-41` says the snapped configuration was already nearly exactly feasible —
but nothing here rules out a *better* tight structure near the same float configuration.
