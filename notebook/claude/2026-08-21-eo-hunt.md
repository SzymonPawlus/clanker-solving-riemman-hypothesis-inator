# 2026-08-21 — Erdős–Oler counterexample hunt (Prover F)

Branch `claude/circle-equklatetal-problem-sa7tx7`. Role: attack the conjecture from the
refutation side, which nobody in this repo had tried. Everything below is `numerical` unless it
says otherwise.

## Framing I settled on before writing code

Separation-1 normalisation, unit-triangle search normalisation: place $n$ points in the unit
equilateral triangle maximising the minimum pairwise distance $m$; then the separation-1 side is
$a = 1/m$. With $\Delta(k) = k(k+1)/2$,

> **EO($k$) is false iff $m(\Delta(k)-1) > 1/(k-1)$.**

That turns the whole thing into one scalar comparison per $k$, which is what I wanted — the
factor-of-2 between this repo's separation-2 certificates ($d = 2a$) and the separation-1
literature is the obvious place to fool yourself, and pinning one normalisation early is the
cheapest defence.

## The exact gate came first, and it is pure Q

Best decision of the session. Barycentric coordinates $(\ell_0,\ell_1,\ell_2)$ w.r.t. the
triangle: containment is *literally* $\ell_i \ge 0$, no $\sqrt3$ anywhere, and for the difference
$u = \ell^P - \ell^Q$ (so $\sum u_i = 0$),

$$|P-Q|^2 = -a^2\sum_{i<j}u_iu_j = \tfrac{a^2}{2}\left(u_0^2+u_1^2+u_2^2\right),$$

because $(\sum u_i)^2 = 0$. Rational $\ell$ and rational $a^2$ ⟹ every squared distance is
rational. So the refutation test is a single exact rational comparison
$q_{\min} > 1/(k-1)^2$ where $q_{\min} = \min_{i<j}(u_0^2+u_1^2+u_2^2)/2$. No intervals, no
$\sqrt3$, no tolerance.

Second, independent checker over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$ (Cartesian, exact sign of $p+q\sqrt3$ by
squaring) written from the geometry rather than from the formula above, per problem `RULES.md` §3.

## Validation, in the order I did it

1. `run.py validate` — the **proven** cases $k=2..6$ ($n=2,5,9,14,20$). All returned $m=1/(k-1)$
   to ≥ 15 digits and the exact gate returned $a_{\min} = k-1$ *exactly*, never below. If the
   optimiser had "beaten" a proven case the attack would have stopped there.
2. A scare, and the lesson. I spot-checked uniform-start success rates against reference $d(n)$
   values I typed **from memory** and got "best 0.2518 vs reference 0.1694" at $n=13$ — apparently
   a 50% improvement on the world record. The optimiser was right and my memory was wrong:
   $d(13) = 0.251813236653061$ in `experiments/circle-packing-search/reference.py`. Exactly the
   §4 failure ordering — misread normalisation before genuine discovery. **Never hand-type a
   reference value.** After re-checking against the file, the pipeline reproduces
   $d(13), d(17), d(19), d(27), d(28)$ to 12+ digits.
3. The real validation is $n = 26$ (`control_n26.py`). $n=26$ is the shape a counterexample would
   have: an irregular packing that genuinely sits below side 6 ($d(26) > 1/6$). It tests both the
   search's power to find such a thing and the **positive** branch of the exact gate, which
   nothing else exercises — a gate that has only ever answered "no" is not evidence.

## What the landscape looks like

Immediately striking, and it cuts both ways: at $n = \Delta(k)-1$ roughly 95% of *all* local
solves land on exactly $1/(k-1)$, from every seed family including uniform random. The lattice
basin is enormous. But at $n=26$, 0/60 uniform starts reach $d(26)$ — the irregular optimum only
5e-5 above $1/6$ is hard to find. So a high hit-rate at $n=27$ is **not** evidence of thorough
coverage; it is evidence that the lattice attractor swallows most starts. That asymmetry is the
main caveat on the whole negative result and I want it in the writeup, not buried.

## The one thing I did not expect

I was told to run $k = 8,9,10$ on the theory that "a counterexample at large $k$ is as good as one
at $k = 7$ and the search space is differently shaped". Halfway through, the arithmetic said the
opposite and I think it is the most useful thing this lane produced.

Trivially $m(n) \le m(n-1)$ (delete a point). So any counterexample to EO($k$) has
$1/(k-1) < m(\Delta(k)-1) \le m(\Delta(k)-2)$, and its side is confined to
$[1/m(\Delta(k)-2),\,k-1)$. Plug in the published $d(\Delta(k)-2)$:

| $k$ | window for $a$ | width | width/(k−1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | [3.971197, 4) | 2.88e-2 | 7.2e-3 |
| 6 | [4.991976, 5) | 8.02e-3 | 1.6e-3 |
| 7 | [5.997419, 6) | 2.58e-3 | 4.3e-4 |
| 8 | [6.999387, 7) | 6.13e-4 | 8.8e-5 |

The room collapses by ~4× per increment of $k$. Large $k$ is not a differently-shaped opportunity,
it is a **smaller** one. If EO is false anywhere the most room is at $k = 7$, and even there the
target is a $2.6\times10^{-3}$ interval of side length out of 6.

Conditional on the records being optimal, of course — that is the weak point and it is stated as
such in the writeup. But the qualitative collapse survives a lot of error in any one row.

Corollary I want the next worker to notice: at $k=8$ the whole window is $1.25\times10^{-5}$ wide
in $m$. Any optimiser without an exact gate will "refute" Erdős–Oler at $k=8$ roughly whenever it
feels like it.

## Things that cost me time, for next time

- `corner_counts` with **closed** corner triangles is the wrong statistic. On the lattice the
  closed triangle of side 1 holds 3 points (corner plus both edge neighbours at distance exactly 1),
  not $T(1)=1$; the eo-hull-deficit §9 condition is about the **open** triangle, where the lattice
  gives exactly $T(j)$. Recomputed with the open convention in `analyze.py`.
- The naive "best local optimum strictly below $1/(k-1)$" is a junk statistic: it is dominated by
  incompletely converged copies of the lattice sitting $10^{-8}$ below it. Replaced with a ladder of
  exclusion radii, which actually shows where distinct structures begin.
- Bash tool calls time out at 2 minutes; long runs must be backgrounded with logs, and the logs need
  `PYTHONUNBUFFERED=1` or nothing appears until the process exits.

## Verification story, assembled deliberately

Three exact checkers, each built from a different observation, plus two controls in opposite
directions. That combination is the whole reason a null result here is worth writing down.

- **Checker 1** barycentric over $\mathbb{Q}$: containment is $\ell_i \ge 0$;
  $|P-Q|^2 = \tfrac{a^2}{2}\sum u_i^2$.
- **Checker 2** Cartesian over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$: sign of $p+q\sqrt3$ by squaring.
- **Checker 3** the simplest of the three, and I wish I had thought of it first: in the triangle
  $(0,0),(L,0),(L/2,L\sqrt3/2)$ with $L$ rational, every rational convex combination is
  $(x, t\sqrt3)$ with $x,t$ rational, so $\sqrt3$ divides out of all three edge inequalities by
  hand and $|P-Q|^2 = \Delta x^2 + 3\Delta t^2$. Everything is a rational comparison.
- **Positive control**: $n = 26$ must be certified as fitting below side 6 — all three agree, at
  the exact side $299871/50000$.
- **Negative control**: the $T(7)$ lattice minus apex shrunk by $\varepsilon$ must be rejected for
  every $\varepsilon > 0$. It is, down to $\varepsilon = 10^{-12}$, where the infeasibility is
  $2\times10^{-12}$ in squared distance — invisible to any tolerance-based check.

If I had only ever run the gate on candidates it rejected, I would have learned nothing about the
gate. The pair of controls is what makes "no candidate passed" mean something.

## Outcome

No counterexample. 30 830 local optima in the census plus 4 672 targeted insertions at
$k = 7,8,9,10$; none exceeded $1/(k-1)$. Rationalised at denominator $10^8$, the best configuration
at every $k$ has $q_{\min}$ **exactly** $1/(k-1)^2$ — it *is* the lattice minus a point, certified
in $\mathbb{Q}$. Kill-criterion K1 (budget) met; K2 (a clean structural gap below the lattice value)
**not** met, and I said so rather than claiming the more impressive version — §5b shows the values
trailing off continuously at $10^{-6}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-3}$ below the threshold, so I cannot rule out
a basin that more compute would reach.

Two things came out that I did not plan and that I think outlast the null:

1. **The counterexample window** (§7). Rigorously, $m(n) \le m(n-1)$, so a violation of EO($k$)
   lives in $a \in [1/m(\Delta(k)-2), k-1)$. Conditional on the published records, that interval's
   relative width falls by ~4× per increment of $k$: $4.3\times10^{-4}$ at $k=7$,
   $8.8\times10^{-5}$ at $k=8$. Refutation gets *harder* with $k$, not differently-easier.
2. **No rotated lattice beats $T(k)$ minus a point** (§6b). Fell out of a sanity check: a hex
   lattice at spacing $\approx 1/(k-1)$ at random rotation does not even *hold* $\Delta(k)-1$ points,
   because the aligned lattice's boundary points sitting exactly on the edges are worth ~10 points
   at $k=7$. Scanning $(\theta,\text{offset})$, the best spacing is $1/(k-1)$ at $\theta = 0$ for
   all four $k$. That kills the most obvious structural candidate for a counterexample.

The honest caveat I keep coming back to: ~95% of solves land on the lattice, so the hit rate is a
measure of the attractor, not of coverage. The $n=26$ control is the proof of that — 0/60 uniform
starts found an optimum only $7\times10^{-5}$ above $1/6$.
