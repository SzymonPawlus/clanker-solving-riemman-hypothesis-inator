# 2026-08-22 — worker C2 — do non-convex / curved pieces help the $n=16$ covering bound?

Attack directory: `problems/circle-packing-equilateral-triangle/attacks/n16-shapes/`.
Code: `experiments/packing-n16-shapes/`.

## What I was sent to do

Break the self-imposed "convex polygons only" restriction on the 15-piece covering lower bound
for $a_{16}$: corner sectors, Reuleaux/constant-width boundary pieces, non-convex L- and
crescent-shaped interior pieces, overlapping pieces. The standing record was
$a_{16} \ge 446335/99998 = 4.4634392688$ (after taking the free dilation), the ceiling
$4.6247636$.

## What actually happened

**The premise is empty, and proving that took ten minutes and no compute.** Two elementary
reductions:

1. $\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{conv} S) = \operatorname{diam}(S)$ — so a non-convex piece
   can always be replaced by its convex hull.
2. A compact set $K$ of diameter $D$ sits inside $Q \oplus E$ where $Q$ is the convex hull of a
   finite $\varepsilon$-net of $K$ (diameter $\le D$, since a hull's diameter is attained at
   generating points) and $E$ a regular hexagon of inradius $\varepsilon$; that is a convex
   **polygon** of diameter $\le D + 4\varepsilon/\sqrt3$.

Together: at **every** side length $a$, coverability by $N$ arbitrary sets of diameter $<1$ is
equivalent to coverability by $N$ convex polygons of diameter $<1$. Not asymptotically —
at the same $a$. So sectors, Reuleaux pieces, disks and non-convex pieces cannot beat a polygon
covering, ever. I wrote this up as Theorem SI.

The manager's follow-up correction (that the isodiametric cap $\pi/4$, not $3\sqrt3/8$, is the
truth, so rounder pieces carry more area) is right about the cap and wrong about the conclusion:
**a polygon can be as round as you like.** The regular $(2m+1)$-gon of diameter 1 has area
$\to \pi/4$. "Round" and "curved" are different properties and only the first one buys area.

## The corner, since that was the named lead

Lemma S: the $60^\circ$ sector of radius $r$ has diameter *exactly* $r$ (the two arc endpoints
realise it), so it is a legal piece of area $\pi r^2/6$. True, exact, and already being used:
control C6 shows the standing certificate's three corner cells **are** the inscribed 2-chord
sector polygon, area $\tfrac12 D^2$ against the sector's $\tfrac{\pi}{6}D^2$. The whole remaining
corner headroom is $3(\pi/6) - 1.49964 = 0.0712$ of area, i.e. $\Delta a \lesssim 0.018$, about
$11\%$ of the $0.161$ that separates the record from the ceiling. So the corner is real but small,
and it is *not* where the missing $0.16$ lives.

Audit of the standing certificate (exact rational, `audit.py`): the area deficit against
$15 \times 3\sqrt3/8$ is $1.1165$, split corners $0.449$ / **edges $0.610$** / interior $0.058$.
The edge collar is the expensive part, and no per-piece inequality sees it — a boundary piece can
have area up to $\frac{\pi-\sqrt3}{2} = 0.7048$ (Reuleaux triangle on the side) while covering a
whole unit of side, well above the hexagon figure. The obstruction is *fitting*, not shape.

## The one freedom left, and what it was worth

Theorem SI leaves exactly one thing the previous attacks did not have: **overlap**, equivalently
partitions into non-convex cells (a covering by convex sets and a partition into arbitrary sets are
the same object). I built a representation for that — a triangulation of $T_a$ whose triangles are
**grouped**, each group's piece being the convex hull of its triangles, so coverage is automatic and
a group whose triangles form a non-convex region gives a piece that overlaps its neighbours.
Seeded from the record, refined so each corner group gets extra arc vertices (going from the
2-chord to the 4-chord sector polygon), which a convex partition cannot do because it would need a
separate neighbouring cell per chord.

Result: a genuine overlapping covering (6 of 105 piece pairs overlap, total overlap area $0.0361$)
certified exactly at $a_{16} \ge 4.463841$ — an improvement of $4.0\times10^{-4}$ over the standing
record. The matched control (same optimiser, same budget, convex partition, no overlap) reached
$4.46378$. **The difference is inside the noise of the optimiser**, so I am not claiming overlap
helped; what I am claiming is that it does not hurt and that it is the only structural freedom
left.

## For whoever picks this up

Do not spend another session on piece shape. Two things are worth doing:

1. **Edge collar.** 55% of the loss. The question is the one-dimensional one: how efficiently can
   diameter-$<1$ convex pieces tile a straight strip while each covering $\approx 0.9$ of the
   boundary line? That is a small, self-contained optimisation and it is where the money is.
2. **Different topologies.** Every run in this repo, mine included, lands in $[4.4634, 4.4640]$
   from the same 3-corner/9-edge/3-interior combinatorics. The plateau is a property of that
   topology, not of the problem. A search that *enumerates* boundary/interior splits (e.g.
   3/10/2, 3/11/1, 3/12/0) would say whether the plateau is real.
