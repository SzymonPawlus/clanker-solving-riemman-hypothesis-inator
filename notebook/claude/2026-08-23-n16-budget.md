## The mathematics, in one line

Lemma S knows only $k_e\ge3$ and must maximise over $(k_1,k_2,k_3)$; its maximiser is $(4,4,4)$,
paying $12\hat f(\frac{a-2}{4})$ at $\ell\approx0.73$. Theorem N's class structure pins the counts,
and I close the argument the rest of the way: the three two-side pieces *are* the vertex pieces, so
they lie in $B(V,1)$ and miss the edge middles; hence $M_e$ is contained in the three one-side
pieces of that side, $k_e\le3$; Lemma S's trace count gives $k_e\ge3$; so $k_e=3$, and the budget
becomes $3\cdot\frac\pi6 + 9\hat f(\frac{a-2}{3}) + 3\cdot\frac\pi4$ at $\ell\approx0.93$.

## Three things worth remembering

1. **My independently written code reproduced the other lane's U2 $=4.914308$ and X1 $=4.836854$
   to six decimals.** That is the strongest single piece of evidence that either pipeline is
   right, and it is also a coupling: if that lane is wrong, this lane is wrong the same way.
2. **The binding lower bound on $f$ changed character when the operating point moved.** At
   $\ell\approx0.70$ (unpinned) the cut disk wins; at $\ell\approx0.93$ (pinned) it collapses and
   the *lens* value $f(1)=\pi/3-\sqrt3/4$, transported by monotonicity of $f$, is what binds.
   Using the other lane's `ceiling.py` logic unchanged would have understated X1′ by $\approx0.03$
   — a case where copying a formula that was correct in its own regime would have been wrong here.
3. **First attempt hung, and the reason is a repo-wide trap.** The certified enclosures carry
   thousand-digit denominators; combined with 64 halvings of a `Fraction` bracket, exact arithmetic
   ground to a halt. Fix: re-round every quantity to $10^{-24}$ **in the safe direction** and
   bisect over integers $m$ with $a=m/10^7$. Rigour is unaffected; runtime went from "never" to
   seconds. Worth knowing before the next lane bisects with `Fraction`s.
