# 2026-08-24 — r5-n17: auditing our own 12-of-17 discrepancy

**Task.** `attacks/r4-famcert/README.md` §1 flagged that the `r3-qsqrt3` `n = 17` certificate and
the four-grain generator at `j = 3` share only 12 of 17 points at the same `s = 6 + 4√3`, and said
this "should not be waved past". Reproduce it, verify both, explain it.

## What I did

Rebuilt the whole stack rather than reusing either author's: my own `Q(√3)` (`Fraction` pairs,
exact sign rule — the one place a lazy implementation reaches for a float), my own exact-expression
parser (rejects decimal strings and anything outside the field), my own checker written from
problem `RULES.md` §2, my own transcription of the generator from its *docstring spec*. Validated
the checker on `n = 3, 4, 6, 10` proven optima and five negative controls **before** pointing it at
anything, including the control that matters — `s` inflated by 1 must be *accepted but reported
not tight*.

## What came out

Headline first, because the kill-criterion was "either configuration is broken": **neither is**.
All six point sets (cert and generator, `n = 17, 24, 31`) are exactly feasible, exactly tight,
min squared distance exactly 4. So this is a third implementation agreeing with `r3-qsqrt3` and
`r3-recheck` on every quantity (26/45/59 contacts, 11/15/18 boundary points, `d_min`). It promotes
nothing — same model family — but it is one more independent seat at the table.

Then the actual question, and the three cases came apart cleanly:

- **`n = 24`**: identical, `24/24`. New fact: that configuration is invariant under the *whole*
  `D₃`. Of course an optimiser and a lattice construction agree there.
- **`n = 31`**: differs in exactly one point. It is a rattler and its free region is exactly the
  segment `{(x,0) : 6 ≤ x ≤ 4+2√3}` on the bottom edge — the generator parks it at the jammed left
  endpoint (picking up 2 contacts, kernel 0), the certificate leaves it interior (kernel 1). Same
  packing, rattler parked differently. Exactly the story famcert §1 hoped for.
- **`n = 17`**: not a symmetry (all 6 isometries of the fixed triangle tested exactly, none maps
  one to the other), not a rattler (cert has exactly 1, generator has 0 — the `r3-qsqrt3` count is
  *right*, and one rattler really cannot explain five points), not a deformation (generator's is
  infinitesimally rigid, kernel 0; cert's kernel is 2, precisely the rattler's translations).
  They are **two different packings at the same `s`**, separated by 26 vs 28 contacts, 11 vs 12
  boundary points, non-isomorphic contact graphs.

So: benign, and now explained. Flag resolved + narrowed, not escalated.

## What I nearly got wrong

My first rattler definition was "no contact **and** strictly interior to all three walls", which is
the natural reading of the problem `RULES.md` gloss. It reports **0 rattlers at `n = 31`** — because
the committed rattler sits *on* wall `AB` at `(7,0)` and rattles by sliding along the edge. For a
few minutes that looked like a contradiction with `attacks/r3-qsqrt3/README.md` §2's table (which
says 1). It is not: that README's "strict slack in every wall constraint" is a statement about the
optimiser's **float** position, and the snap moved the point onto the wall. Every *number* in that
table is correct. But the definitional gap is real, and a future rattler-census script will trip
on it, so it is written up (attack README §4.2) rather than silently patched around. Adding the
rigidity-matrix rank was what settled it — kernel 1 is not a matter of opinion.

The other thing worth naming: I checked that my re-transcribed generator emits the same points as
theirs for `j = 0..5` before comparing anything. If I had silently mis-transcribed a grain range I
would have been auditing a configuration that no one had ever claimed.

## Cost

Well inside the hour. The expensive-looking part (exact Gaussian elimination over `Q(√3)` on a
82×62 rigidity matrix) is trivial; the exact bisection for free radii is the slow bit and it is
still seconds.
